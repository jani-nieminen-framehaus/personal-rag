# Exposure

Exposure is the amount of light that reaches the sensor. Three controls shape
it: aperture, shutter speed, and ISO. Together they form the exposure
triangle — change one, and at least one of the others has to compensate to
preserve the same overall brightness.

## Exposure compensation

Exposure compensation (EC) lets you bias the camera's metering away from
"what the meter thinks is correct." Use it when the meter is fooled — backlit
subjects, snow, dark scenes with small bright areas.

- In golden hour, -1/3 EV is a common starting point for skin tones.
- For snow scenes, +1 to +1.5 EV keeps the snow white instead of grey.
- The histogram is the source of truth; the meter is a guess.

## Metering modes

Most cameras offer matrix/evaluative, center-weighted, and spot metering.
Matrix is the default and the right call 90% of the time. Switch to spot when
the scene has a wide dynamic range and you want to lock exposure to a
specific tone (e.g. a face, a highlight).

# Low light

When ambient light falls below the lens's comfortable working range, raise
ISO before slowing the shutter below 1/(focal length × crop factor). For a
50mm lens on APS-C, that's ~1/80s hand-held.

Stabilization helps but isn't free — modern IBIS buys you 3–5 stops with a
steady hand. Combine with a fast prime (f/1.4 or f/1.8) and you can usually
keep ISO under 6400 for indoor work.
